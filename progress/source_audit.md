## Updated targeted gap check

Searches on 20 October 2026 combined “course registration”, “waitlist”, “queue position”, “visibility”, “switching”, “information disclosure”, “experiment”, and “student behavior”. This is a targeted search, not a systematic review.

A closer adjacent lead is **The Effect of Course Shutouts on Community College Students: Evidence from Waitlist Cutoffs**, [IZA discussion-paper record](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/13822/the-effect-of-course-shutouts-on-community-college-students-evidence-from-waitlist-cutoffs). It uses administrative registration data and waitlist admission discontinuities to examine course shutouts. It is relevant evidence on the consequences of course-access constraints, but the retrieved abstract does not identify an experiment varying the visibility of queue rank. It should not be represented as direct evidence of a visibility effect without inspecting the full paper.

Institutional waitlist guidance also demonstrates that mechanisms differ: [University of Connecticut](https://registrar.uconn.edu/wait-lists/) describes confirmation of waitlist membership without position disclosure, while [Berkeley Mathematics](https://math.berkeley.edu/node/561) describes periodic position-based processing and section constraints. These are procedures, not behavioural evaluations, and are not added as research findings.

The Stanford search result **Who Loses Out? Registration Order, Course Availability, and Student Behaviors in Community College** is another adjacent lead, but its [former institutional URL](https://cepa.stanford.edu/content/who-loses-out-registration-order-course-availability-and-student-behaviors-community-college) redirected to a general archive during verification; do not cite an uninspected result as established evidence.

Defensible wording: **“Our targeted search found limited direct evidence on the joint effect of personal queue-position visibility, timestamped FCFS ordering, forfeiture of a surrendered position, and repeated university course-choice revision. We therefore examine that combination in a bounded synthetic model.”** Do not claim exhaustive coverage, novelty as the first study, or absence of all related course-waitlist research.
